# Candidates — Make the AI Judge Laugh: Office Jokes (v2)

Scored honestly 1–5 on the bounty judge criteria, in order:

1. Genuine one-line joke about office life, not a slogan or observation
2. Real turn or surprise at the end, landing somewhere unexpected
3. Recognition — the truth of the office, so the laugh comes with a wince
4. Tight wording, every word earning its place
5. Freshness — not the obvious Monday morning or photocopier line

Strict standard: a 25/25 must be genuine on every criterion, especially
freshness. Scores below are my honest judgement, not a sales pitch.

## Why each score

- **#1 (v1, polished as-is, 24/25):** The control line. Setup, escalation
  and HR-file landing are all strong, and the wording is tight. Freshness
  stays 4/5: reply-all + HR is a familiar pairing, and the joke leans on a
  well-worn office trope even though the execution is sharp.
- **#2 (22/25):** Strong recognition — everyone has hidden behind a status
  to do real work. The turn (the work is from the meeting itself) is clever
  but lands a little soft, and "actually" is a spare word. Slack-status jokes
  are also fairly common, so freshness is 4/5.
- **#3 (24/25):** "As discussed" is the office's favourite bluff,
  instantly recognised. The turn is a double escalation: the conversation
  never happened, and the bluff did not just pass — it became policy. That
  is a genuinely unexpected landing, and it stings because it is true:
  undocumented claims harden into rules. At 83 characters nothing is spare;
  cutting "in an email" from an earlier draft made it tighter without
  losing the setup. Freshness is 4/5: it avoids Monday, the photocopier,
  the standing desk, "per my last email", the calendar, the plant, the
  thermostat, the fire drill and unlimited PTO, and the premise (a phrase
  inventing its own history) is not a standard joke setup.
  Note: superseded by the polish line in main.py — the thumbs-up emoji budget joke is now the entry.
- **#4 (22/25):** Dark and fresh, and the finance-approval twist works. But
  it carries two punches (finance approved it / HR wants the manager), which
  muddies the landing and costs tightness (3/5). One twist would have been
  stronger than two.
- **#5 (20/25):** A neat aphorism, but it is closer to an observation than a
  joke: there is no real setup-and-turn, the claim itself is the whole line.
  Criterion 2 is 3/5 and criterion 1 is 4/5 for that reason.
- **#6 (24/25):** The closest challenger. Promoting the silence, then giving
  it a parking spot, is a genuinely surprising double escalation and very
  fresh. It loses one point on tightness (4/5): the parking-spot beat is a
  second joke riding on the first, and the line is fractionally busier than
  #3. In a pairwise contest I expect #3's single clean turn to edge it.
- **#7 (22/25):** The "highest-paid slice" pun lands well and the wording is
  tight, but pizza-instead-of-a-raise is one of the most-told office
  grievances on the internet. Freshness is 3/5, and that caps it.
- **#8 (20/25):** A nice meta loop (the job is replying to the email about
  the guide), but at 112 characters it is the wordiest line here and the
  setup takes too long to pay off. Tightness 3/5, freshness 4/5.

## Winner

**#3 — "I wrote 'as discussed' about a conversation we never had — it's now company policy." (83 characters) — revised to 24/25 (freshness 4/5); superseded by the polish line in main.py.**

It was the leading line because it scored a 5 on four of the five criteria at
once: a real joke with a setup and a punch, a surprise landing (a lie
becoming policy), universal recognition ("as discussed"), nothing wasted
in 83 characters, and a premise that is not one of the obvious office
lines. #6 came closest; #1 remains the proven 24/25 fallback.
